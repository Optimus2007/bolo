# Teach Myna all twenty commands first

*Research report, 2026-11-12. Written after the first real-voice test: one speaker (the project owner), 55 clips, laptop
mic. Numbers from that test are in [`results/speech/human_v1.json`](../../results/speech/human_v1.json). The recordings
themselves never leave the owner's laptop and are not in this repository. Earlier research:
[IMPROVING_SPEECH_ACCURACY.md](IMPROVING_SPEECH_ACCURACY.md). Some sources are 2026 preprints read as abstracts; the
report says where.*

Myna's biggest real-voice problem is coverage, not acoustics. The speech model was fine-tuned on only 9 of the 20 commands. It gets 73% of actions right on those 9 and 48% on the 11 it never heard, and a weighted split of the 61.8% headline implies that roughly 25 of the 55 clips fall in that untrained 48% group. Regenerating Indic Parler TTS data for all 20 commands, plus free-form paraphrases, costs one Kaggle session. That alone should lift overall action accuracy toward the low 70s, before any real-speech trick is applied. Three moves follow, in order. First, harden the pipeline after the ASR: phonetic matching of command verbs and entities, an NLU trained on Bolo Sun's own N-best errors, and an abstention layer whose gates (voice-activity detection, a duration-based token cap, a speaking-rate check, a grammar check, slot verification) would each have stopped "cpu cpu cpu" on its own. Second, apply the best-supported synthetic-to-real fixes, which are real room impulse responses, noise, roughly ten voices from a better TTS engine and a little real speech in every batch. Third, add a personal LoRA adapter trained on the M2 from 150-250 of the user's own clips. In comparable Google and Whisper studies that step gave 50-75% relative WER reductions, but it should come after the coverage fix, or it will spend the user's recordings teaching vocabulary that TTS can supply for free. One warning frames all of this. At n=55 the 95% interval on 61.8% runs from 48.6% to 73.5%, so any gain smaller than about six net clips fixed is indistinguishable from noise. A disciplined evaluation protocol is therefore part of the plan, not an afterthought.

## The 55 clips point at coverage, free-form speech and the decoder's price

The first real-voice run says more than the headline number. Here are the three configurations measured on the same 55 clips (40 read, 15 free-form) from the project's own results file ([human_v1.json](../../results/speech/human_v1.json)).

| Config | Correct action | Read | Free-form | Intent acc | Wrong action | Over-abstain | Read WER / CER | Mean latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bolo Sun, beam 5 + rerank | 61.8% | 70% | 40% | 74.5% | 20.0% | 19.2% | 30.8% / 15.9% | 225 ms |
| Bolo Sun, greedy | 52.7% | 60% | 33% | 67.3% | 18.2% | 30.8% | 35.7% / 19.2% | 108 ms |
| Oriserve Swift base, greedy | 50.9% | 55% | 40% | 63.6% | 20.0% | 30.8% | 31.3% / 12.6% | 135 ms |

Four readings matter. **The 73% versus 48% split on trained versus untrained commands is the largest effect in the data.** Solving 0.618 = 0.73x + 0.48(1−x) gives x ≈ 0.55, so about 30 clips used trained commands and 25 did not. If the untrained group reached the trained group's 73%, overall accuracy would be about 73%, roughly six more correct clips. That is an arithmetic projection, not a measurement. It also matches the literature: in the DAS pipeline (LLM-written domain text, then TTS, then LoRA on Whisper), domain-matched synthetic text cut real-speech WER by 10-17% relative in new domains, with only about 1% regression elsewhere ([Tran et al., arXiv 2501.12501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12501)). A narrowly fine-tuned decoder is effectively a command language model, and a command it never saw gets pulled toward words it did see. The "cpu cpu cpu" loop for "sakshi ko whatsapp karo" fits this pattern, with an in-vocabulary token repeating on an unfamiliar sentence.

**Free-form speech is the second gap.** Read prompts reach 70% and free-form commands 40%. On free-form the fine-tune is no better than the untouched base model (40% each). The synthetic text was template-like, and real users paraphrase.

**The fine-tune bought command form at the cost of acoustic fidelity.** On read clips the base model has the lower character error rate (12.6% vs 15.9%) and about the same WER, while Bolo Sun gets far more sentences exactly right (42.5% vs 17.5%). That pattern suggests the fine-tune learned the command phrasing but drifted acoustically on real voices. Interpolating weights between the base and fine-tuned models therefore deserves a fresh sweep on real audio. The earlier 50/50 WiSE-FT test was scored on synthetic voices and OpenSLR-104, not on human command audio.

**Beam 5 is worth 9 points but breaks the latency budget.** It adds 9 points of action accuracy over greedy, but a 225 ms mean is already above the 100-200 ms target. Beam search also has a hallucination cost. On speech padded with non-speech, beam 5 raised detected hallucinations from 19.5-20.3% (beam 1) to 28.2-37.4% in Whisper large-v3 ([Barański et al., arXiv 2501.11378](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378v1)). Whatever replaces raw beam width has to be cheaper and safer.

Finally, a **20% wrong-action rate** is the number users will remember. One wrong action in five is worse than a 19% over-abstain rate, because a refusal costs a retry while a wrong WhatsApp message costs trust. Every ranking below weighs wrong actions more heavily than missed ones.

## Synthetic-to-real gains come from coverage, rooms and a little real speech, not more Parler voices

After fixing command coverage, the literature agrees on what closes the rest of the TTS-to-human gap, and it is not more speakers from the same engine. The closest study, Whisper-Small on Hindi, Kannada and Telugu, found that synthetic-only training (about 165 h) reached 0.427 average Hindi WER against 0.336 for real Vaani speech. Real plus synthetic was best at 0.285, a 15.1% relative gain over real alone ([Pulikodan et al., arXiv 2606.17662](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17662v1)). The same paper bears directly on Bolo's choices. **Indic Parler TTS tied for the worst of three engines** (0.685 WER, against 0.601 for IndriTTS), and **the gains from voice cloning stopped at about 10 voices**: 1 voice 0.613, 10 voices 0.546, 100 voices 0.667, 1,000 voices 0.645 ([arXiv 2606.17662](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17662v1)). Attention encoder-decoder models like Whisper show the largest synthetic-to-real gap of any ASR family ([Rossenbach et al., arXiv 2407.17997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17997)), which makes Bolo Sun's 82%-to-62% drop typical rather than a bug.

**Room impulse responses are the best-supported augmentation for TTS audio.** They remove the studio-clean signature that real laptop microphones never produce. In an LLM-ASR study, raw synthetic data never beat the real-only baseline (8.68% WER) at any mixing ratio. RIR-convolved synthetic data did, reaching 8.45% at 70/30 real/synthetic and 8.01% when the full real set was combined with the full RIR-augmented set. Even 10% real plus 90% RIR-synthetic (10.26%) beat the unadapted model (10.90%). Time-stretching without RIR slightly hurt ([arXiv 2606.29031](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29031)). SynthASR's production recipe applied reverb from 10k measured RIRs with 60% probability and noise at 10-20 dB SNR with an independent 60% probability, plus SpecAugment, and mixed real into every batch at 95:5 then 98:2. It improved a new domain by more than 65% relative with no general-domain loss ([Fazel et al., ar5iv 2106.07803](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.07803)). Pitch augmentation and voice-conversion speaker augmentation were ineffective in an exhaustive comparison ([Ogun et al., arXiv 2503.08954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08954)), so Parler's description-driven pitch variety is unlikely to be the missing ingredient. OpenSLR 28 (real and simulated RIRs plus point-source noises) is Apache 2.0 ([OpenSLR 28](https://openslr.org/28)), and `audiomentations` applies all of this on the fly at negligible CPU cost for 5k clips.

**Real speech in the batch is the lever no augmentation replaces.** The user's 55 clips were recorded as `evaluation_only: true`; on 2026-11-12 the user additionally allowed local training of their own personal model (recorded in the local speakers file), but never a shared model and never publication. So for the general model the usable real data is public. MUCS 2021 is the one sizable real Hindi-English code-switched corpus, with about 90 h of lecture-style speech under CC BY-SA 4.0 ([OpenSLR 104](https://www.openslr.org/104/)). IndicVoices (CC BY 4.0, 74% extempore speech that naturally contains English words) ([HF IndicVoices](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicVoices)) and Svarah, Indian-accented English for words like "volume" and "wifi" (CC BY 4.0) ([HF Svarah](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/Svarah)), cover the rest. Because those corpora are mostly in Devanagari, either label them with the base Oriserve model's own Roman output, which also behaves like distillation toward the base, or use them only to protect the encoder. Oversample whatever real speech exists to 10-30% of each batch. With small real sets, oversampling matters more than matching the published ratios.

**The choice of fine-tuning method is less settled than the data question.** On Whisper code-switching, full fine-tuning degraded the model while LoRA stayed near baseline ([arXiv 2506.21576](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21576)). In a Pacific-language study, however, full fine-tuning was the more stable choice ([arXiv 2603.06310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06310)), and smaller Whisper models forget readily even under LoRA with a frozen encoder ([arXiv 2408.10680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10680)). Replay is the one safeguard that holds across studies. With LoRA, multi-domain experience replay cut forgetting by 47% ([arXiv 2512.16401](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16401v3)), and a Hindi telephony adaptation reported 18.2% relative target-WER gain with 54% less forgetting ([arXiv 2601.18266](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18266)). For the retrain, use replay of real general speech plus a KL penalty toward the base model, then sweep the WiSE-FT interpolation α (θ = (1−α)·θ_base + α·θ_ft) at 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7 on real audio. In vision, WiSE-FT gained 4-6 points under distribution shift at no inference cost ([arXiv 2109.01903](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.01903)). No Whisper-specific WiSE-FT result exists, so the sweep is an experiment, but it takes minutes on the M2.

**A second TTS engine is a medium-cost, medium-gain step.** About 10 cloned voices from IndriTTS, XTTS-v2 or IndicF5 would follow the Indic paper's best configuration, but each engine's license has to be checked first. Add non-speech clips (fan, keyboard, room tone, silence) with empty transcripts to the same run. Calm-Whisper fine-tuned only 3 decoder heads on non-speech audio with blank labels and cut non-speech hallucinations by more than 80%, with under 0.1% WER cost ([arXiv 2505.12969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12969v1)). Bolo cannot reproduce the head selection cheaply, but blank-labelled noise in the training mix targets the same failure.

## A private LoRA adapter on the M2 should cut the user's errors by half or more

Personal adaptation is well supported, fits the M2 comfortably and never needs the audio to leave the laptop. The closest analogue trained Whisper with LoRA for one speaker using HF Transformers and PEFT entirely on an Apple M4 Max, in full precision. The setup was r=8, alpha/r=2, dropout 0.05 on q_proj and v_proj in all encoder and decoder attention layers, LR 4-5e-5, at most 13 epochs, and the best checkpoint chosen by validation WER. It took WER from 49.2% to 17.0% (medium) and from 30.4% to 12.4% (large-v3), measured on 100 sentences written by someone else and recorded on a different day ([Condado & Lobo, arXiv 2611.11327](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11327)). A second single-speaker Whisper study found that LoRA on all attention projections gave "the most reliable accuracy" and the best time-accuracy trade-off ([Giaimis et al., arXiv 2610.24572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24572)). Both used dysarthric speech, which is a harder and larger mismatch than Bolo's TTS-to-human gap, so relative gains could be smaller here.

**The encoder must be in the adapter.** Google's Euphonia work found that fine-tuning only the 2 layers closest to the input recovered 91% of the full fine-tuning gain on non-standard speech and 86% on accented speech ([Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/project-euphonias-personalized-speech-recognition-for-non-standard-speech/)). A voice-and-microphone mismatch lives on the input side. Decoder-only adaptation would mostly relearn vocabulary, which the TTS coverage fix supplies more cheaply. A reasonable starting configuration for Whisper-base (6+6 layers, d_model 512) is LoRA r=8, alpha 16, dropout 0.1 on q, k, v and out_proj in encoder self-attention and in decoder self- and cross-attention. That is roughly 0.6M trainable parameters, a back-of-envelope figure. Use LR 1e-4 to 5e-4, since base-size models tolerate higher rates than the medium and large models in the cited work. Ablate against q,v-only and encoder-only.

**The data need is small, and the steep part of the curve is where the user stands.** In Google's on-device personalization, 50 short utterances gave a median 71% relative WER reduction, and home-automation task success rose from 40% to 81% ([Tomanek et al., arXiv 2106.10259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10259)). Across 10/30/50/100/250-utterance tiers (0.7 to 18.1 minutes of audio), the share of speakers reaching WER ≤15% rose from 35% unadapted to 63% at 50 utterances and 79% at 250 ([Tobin & Tomanek, arXiv 2110.04612](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.04612)). Bolo's commands are 1-3 s long, so 150 clips is only about 5 minutes of audio, comparable to the 50-100 utterance tiers. **Aim for 200 clips, recorded across at least 3 sessions**, at different times, distances and noise levels. Include free-form paraphrases, since that is where accuracy is lowest. A Nepali Whisper LoRA learning curve went 73.9 → 58.0 → 49.5 → 54.1 → 43.1 WER at 50/100/200/300/403 utterances ([arXiv 2609.01737](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01737v1)). That non-monotonic step is a reminder that one split at this scale can mislead.

**Overfitting control is mostly replay and checkpoint selection.** Mix personal clips 1:1 to 1:3 with replayed synthetic commands, which are already on disk, so the adapter learns the voice and keeps the vocabulary and romanization convention. Augment the personal clips on the fly with speed 0.9-1.1, noise at 5-20 dB, simulated RIRs and SpecAugment. Train 5-15 epochs and keep the best checkpoint on a held-out session. The replay ratio is a heuristic, since no study measured it for single-speaker Whisper. Ship the result as a separate adapter of a few MB that loads only for this user and leaves the base model untouched for anyone else. Naive Whisper LoRA on L2 speech lowered mean WER while widening subgroup disparities ([arXiv 2510.10738](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10738v1)), so check per-intent accuracy and synthetic-test accuracy before and after, not just the mean.

**On MPS, keep everything in fp32.** `fp16=True` with Seq2SeqTrainer errors in Whisper's encoder convolution on an M2 Max ([HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/tensor-types-mismatch-when-trying-to-enable-gpu/43541)). With gradient checkpointing, set `use_reentrant: False` ([HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/runtimeerror-backward-through-graph-with-whisper-medium-and-gradient-checkpointing-true/168571)), and set `PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1`. MLX users can try `mlx-tune`, which exposes Whisper LoRA on the encoder and decoder plus adapter merging ([mlx-tune docs](https://arahim3.github.io/mlx-tune/audio.html)). It is a third-party project and unverified on Whisper-base. A base-size LoRA run on about 200 clips with replay is roughly 4k forward-backward passes. That should take minutes, which makes 5-fold cross-validation with 2-3 seeds practical. This is an estimate, since no measured M2 timings exist.

**Consent is in place for local personal training (given 2026-11-12), but the 55 clips should still stay the lockbox test set.** Training on them would make the user's own numbers meaningless. Record new adaptation clips in separate sessions for training, and keep the 55 for testing only. Test-time adaptation (SUTA, SGEM), which needs no labels, is a later experiment. It adds per-utterance gradient steps to a budget that is already over, and it has little Whisper-specific evidence ([SUTA, arXiv 2203.14222](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.14222)).

## Phonetic lexicons and ASR-noised training make the NLU forgive "goval" and "hodo"

The observed errors ("previous" → "premium", "google" → "goval", "dhundho" → "hodo", "quit arc" → "whit arduino") are exactly the kind the NLU cannot fix from text alone and a closed lexicon can. Bolo has about 20 command verbs and a known list of installed apps, sites and files. **Phonetic nearest-neighbour matching of the verb and slot spans against those lists is the cheapest direct fix.** In an enterprise assistant, adding phonetic features was "the most substantial single jump" in entity resolution on noisy ASR, and character plus phoneme retrieval gave a 40.8% accuracy improvement ([EMNLP 2019 demo D19-3011](https://preview.aclanthology.org/author-url/D19-3011.pdf)). Index each lexicon entry by normalized characters, a Hinglish phonetic key and Double Metaphone, and take the best score with a margin requirement. Every confirmed correction ("goval" → google) can be stored as a personal alias. That is a second, text-only form of on-device personalization. On synthetic voices the project's own alias simulation learned 49-55 aliases with almost no accuracy change (81.7% to 82.1%) ([adapt_sim.json](../../results/speech/adapt_sim.json)), which is expected when there is little error to correct. It needs re-testing on real audio.

**Train Bolo Tiny on Bolo Sun's own mistakes, mixed 1:1 with clean text.** Alexa found that training on ASR hypotheses alone hurt clean-transcript accuracy, while mixing the clean and ASR losses was best on both. Slot F1 on ASR input rose by 0.9% to 10.7% relative across domains, with clean-text changes within ±0.4% ([Ruan et al., Interspeech 2020](https://cdn.amazon.science/86/d9/d47a808442aab85d725b0c469199/towards-an-error-robust-spoken-language-understanding-system.pdf)). On ASR-GLUE, TTS→ASR round-trip augmentation always gave the highest accuracy on error-laden input, and text-level confusion-matrix augmentation was the most balanced. Error-correction post-processors were often useless, because the ASR already embeds a strong language model ([Feng et al., Interspeech 2022](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/feng22c_interspeech.pdf)). Back-transcription research found that swapping TTS audio for real recordings "does not meaningfully change" NLU-robustness conclusions ([Kubis et al., EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.724)), which supports using Bolo's own TTS clips as an error generator. In practice, decode every training clip with all 5 beams, project slot labels through Levenshtein alignment, and add a confusion table seeded from the real errors. Inject those confusions only at verb and slot positions, because generic random swaps match ASR error patterns poorly ([Lee et al., arXiv 2409.06263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.06263)).

**Let the NLU see the whole N-best list, not just score each beam.** Jointly encoding the n-best list cut Alexa domain-classification error by 21.71% relative to a transcript-trained baseline ([ASR N-best Fusion Nets, ICASSP 2021](https://cdn.amazon.science/a2/32/baf51a96489bbb47e86ba8107beb/asr-n-best-fusion-nets.pdf)). Concatenating up to 10 hypotheses with separators matched the state of the art on DSTC2 and helped most in low-data regimes ([Ganesan et al., ACL 2021](https://www.aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.14)). For a 0.7M model the simplest version is averaging pooled encodings across beams, which costs five forward passes of a tiny model.

**Biasing toward English command words needs to be narrow.** The project's trie boost likely failed for identifiable reasons. Gains from Whisper trie fusion appear only on rare and biased words, so the synthetic test set left no headroom. Rewards must apply per token and be cancelled when a match breaks: a final-token-only reward gave 7.95 biased-word WER against 6.11 for uniform per-token reward. Trie entries must match Whisper's exact BPE tokens, including leading spaces and script. With those fixes plus filtered ASR-derived spelling variants, frozen Whisper-large-v3 cut biased-word WER by about 43% with unbiased WER flat. Unfiltered variants were catastrophic, sending unbiased WER to about 21% ([Liu et al., arXiv 2508.17796](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17796v2)). Boost only tokens the model already finds unlikely (log-prob below about −4.0) to avoid over-boosting ([ar5iv 2308.02092](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.02092)). Keep prompt lists short. Plain prompting improved rare-word WER but raised WER on other words, and in German it pushed WER from 6.3 to 15.2 ([Jogi et al., arXiv 2502.11572](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11572v2)). Prompts of comma-separated entity names "may induce hallucination" ([CB-Whisper, arXiv 2309.09552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09552v1)). Given 20 closed command types, the better fit is a constrained or boosted verb slot after a recognized frame, with free-form slots left unconstrained, combined with the phonetic post-match above. No published grammar-constrained Whisper result exists for command-and-control, so this is a design inference.

**Canonicalize every string before scoring and before the NLU.** That means transliterating Devanagari to Roman, lowercasing, collapsing spelling variants (karo/kro, dhundho/dhundo) through a phonetic key, and comparing multiword entities with spaces removed. Spelling and segmentation normalization alone cut measured WER by about 13% on average in four other languages ([arXiv 2303.05046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.05046v1.pdf)). The 61.8% versus 67.3% gap between strict and spacing-lenient action accuracy here shows that Bolo's grounder is losing three clips purely to segmentation.

**Keep the pipeline; do not move to end-to-end SLU.** On the STOP challenge, the ASR→NLU pipeline was "significantly better than all the E2E SLU models", and the winner combined the two approaches ([arXiv 2305.01620](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.01620)). Grounding against installed apps needs a text span anyway. A cheap hybrid is a pooled intent head on the frozen Whisper encoder, used as one more rerank feature that penalizes "premium karo" when the acoustics favour the media intent. Its training audio would be TTS only, so it may inherit the same synthetic-to-real gap.

## Every independent gate would have stopped "cpu cpu cpu"

The repeat rule fixes the one observed loop. The general problem is that Whisper hallucinates with high confidence, so no single score catches it. Hallucinated segments tend to be predicted confidently ([arXiv 2603.06193](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06193)). A logistic regression on Whisper's own fallback heuristics (avg logprob, compression ratio, no-speech probability) detected real-speech hallucinations at only F1 23.6% ([Jasiński et al., arXiv 2606.23060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23060)). The default compression-ratio gate of 2.4 ([openai/whisper transcribe.py](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/transcribe.py)) is nearly useless on short commands. By the zlib formula faster-whisper uses ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/blob/master/faster_whisper/transcribe.py)), "cpu cpu cpu cpu" scores 1.07 and eight repetitions still score only 2.21. Bolo's repeat rule is strictly better at this length. What works is several cheap, independent gates.

**Voice-activity gating is the single biggest measured mitigation.** On Whisper large-v3 with speech mixed with non-speech, WER fell from 104.8% to 8.0% with Silero VAD alone, and to 6.5% with Silero plus delooping plus removal of a bag of known hallucination strings. WebRTC VAD reached only 68.3% ([Barański et al., arXiv 2501.11378](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378v1)). Silero runs on 32 ms frames with threshold 0.5 and a 250 ms minimum speech duration ([silero-vad](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/blob/master/src/silero_vad/utils_vad.py)). Reject clips with less than about 250 ms of speech, and pass only the speech span plus padding to the model. Build a Bolo-specific bag of hallucinations by running Bolo Sun on silence, fan, keyboard and room noise and logging what it says. In the Barański study, 1,270 distinct outputs accounted for 67% of all hallucinations.

**Duration consistency catches loops before they finish.** Characters per second was the best reference-free text feature for hallucination detection, at AUC 68.2% ([arXiv 2606.23060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23060)). Hallucinated segments had more non-vocal time (5.3 s vs 3.5 s on average) ([Koenecke et al., arXiv 2402.08021](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08021v2)), so normalize by VAD-voiced seconds rather than clip length. Cap `max_new_tokens` at roughly k·voiced_seconds + c. That stops runaway loops during decoding and also saves latency, which matters at 225 ms. Add `no_repeat_ngram_size≈3`. No peer-reviewed measurement of repetition penalties on Whisper hallucination exists, so validate both on the real clips. All thresholds must be calibrated on Bolo's own audio, since no Hinglish speaking-rate norms were found.

**The grammar and the device are free verifiers.** A loop like "cpu cpu cpu" should never parse into a well-formed intent with valid slots, and "Sakshi" should be checked against the contact list before anything is sent. Then combine the signals Bolo already computes into one logistic-regression accept score, and fit it on labelled real outcomes:

- length-normalized logprob
- minimum token logprob and mean token entropy
- the top-1/top-2 beam margin
- whether the N-best list agrees on the action
- no-speech probability
- loop and characters-per-second features
- NLU intent probability
- grounding match score

Entropy-based confidence detected incorrect words 2-4 times better than max-probability in CTC and RNN-T models ([Laptev & Ginsburg, arXiv 2212.08703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08703)). A linear probe on mean-pooled middle decoder layers reached about 82% AUC for hallucination detection ([arXiv 2606.23060](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23060)), and those states are computed anyway during decoding. The probe needs labelled real errors, so it belongs after the adaptation recordings exist.

**Abstention should be tiered by risk.** Alexa's design guidance says to "use confirmation sparingly" ([Alexa docs](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/docs/alexa/custom-skills/define-the-dialog-to-collect-and-confirm-required-information.html)), and the voice-UX literature reserves explicit confirmation for error-prone values and high-stakes actions ([Manning, ch. 8](https://www.manning.com/preview/voice-applications-for-alexa-and-google-assistant/chapter-8)). For Myna, that means three tiers:

- Act with an implicit echo for reversible actions such as opening an app or changing volume.
- Ask "Sakshi ko WhatsApp bhejun?" before outward-facing or irreversible actions unless confidence is very high.
- Reprompt whenever a hard gate fires: no speech, a loop, a rate violation, a blocklist hit, N-best disagreement on the action, or an unverifiable slot.

Pick thresholds from a risk-coverage curve so that the wrong-action rate on risky intents approaches zero while confirmations stay rare. The current 20% wrong-action and 19% over-abstain rates show the existing threshold is badly placed on both sides. There is real tension here: tightening thresholds converts wrong actions into refusals, and refusals already run at 19%. Only the accuracy fixes above can shrink both at once.

## With one speaker, only gains of six or more net clips count

Honest evaluation is the constraint that decides whether any of this can be shown to work. **The 95% Wilson interval on 34/55 is 48.6%-73.5%.** At 150/200 correct, it narrows to 68.6%-80.5%. Comparisons must be paired on the same clips, using McNemar's exact test on the clips one system fixed (b) and broke (c) ([Gillick & Cox, ICASSP 1989](https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/3522834.htm)).

| Fixed (b) | Broken (c) | Exact p | Significant at 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 1 | 0.070 | no |
| 8 | 2 | 0.109 | no |
| 6 | 0 | 0.031 | yes |
| 9 | 1 | 0.021 | yes |
| 10 | 2 | 0.039 | yes |

On 55 clips, a net gain of about six to eight is the smallest effect distinguishable from noise. Several items in the plan below will land under that bar individually and should be reported as directional.

For WER, bootstrap utterances to get intervals ([Bisani & Ney, ICASSP 2004](https://www-i6.informatik.rwth-aachen.de/~bisani/bootlog.html)). Clips from one speaker and session are correlated, though, so resample blocks such as sessions or prompt groups, or the intervals will be too narrow and produce false positives ([Liu & Peng, arXiv 1912.09508](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.09508)).

The protocol follows from those numbers:

- **Keep a lockbox.** Freeze the existing 55 clips, which are already evaluation-only, as a test set used once per final candidate. Every extra look spends it, so record a new lockbox session if it gets used for choosing.
- **Tag every new clip.** Record new clips in three or more sessions, each tagged with session, prompt, read/free and intent.
- **Split by prompt and session.** Develop with GroupKFold grouped by prompt text, or better, leave-one-session-out, so no sentence or session appears on both sides. Pool the out-of-fold predictions so every clip counts once, and run 2-3 seeds, because MPS training is not bit-for-bit repeatable.
- **Test both generalisation axes.** Measure accuracy on new prompts and on command types or slot values absent from the personal training clips. The Condado & Lobo claim rests on unseen sentences recorded on a different day ([arXiv 2611.11327](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11327)).
- **Fix the tuning budget in advance.** Set a maximum, such as 10 configurations, and select on cross-validation only.
- **Lead with correct action.** Report correct-action rate first, wrong-action rate second, and canonicalized WER third, split by read and free-form.

A personal adapter and a general model need different evidence. The adapter needs only this user's lockbox. Claims about Myna in general need other speakers. Even a few dozen consented, locally recorded clips from two or three family members would show whether the general fixes transfer and whether the adapter is truly personal, and those clips must stay evaluation-only. Public held-out speech such as the MUCS test split can tune general-model scalars without touching anyone's private audio.

## Conclusion

The first human-voice run shifts the order of work. The earlier research framed Bolo's problem as decoding context and training realism, and both still matter. The sharper finding is that a 25-point accuracy gap between trained and untrained commands outweighs everything else measured, and a free TTS regeneration closes most of it. The second shift concerns trust. A 20% wrong-action rate is a safety number, not an accuracy number, and the fixes for it are deterministic gates that cost no training and should ship before any accuracy work. Personal adaptation is the most powerful lever for this user, and the M2 can run it privately in minutes. Its value is real only if the evaluation can see it, which, at one speaker, means a frozen lockbox and paired tests.

Several things remain genuinely unknown. No published result covers Whisper-base fine-tuned on Hinglish TTS, trie or phonetic biasing on Roman Hinglish, single-speaker LoRA on 1-3 s commands, or the right replay ratio for personal adaptation. The 73%-versus-48% split rests on roughly 25-30 clips per group and could be partly an artifact of which commands happen to be harder. The beam-search latency question is open, since 225 ms already exceeds the budget and no measured cheaper alternative exists.
